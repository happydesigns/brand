Color decisions start with the job of the color, not the shade number. Most screens should be built from Warm White, Graphite, and Sand first; Coral, Peach, Petrol, and the state colors enter only when their role is needed.

::id-brand-reference{section="colors"}
::

## Palette intent

- **Sand:** Neutral foundation for structure. Use for borders, separators, muted surfaces, and the full range of grey-adjacent backgrounds.
- **Coral:** Signature accent. The most expressive color in the system — use sparingly so it retains its brand value.
- **Peach:** Warm atmosphere and identity moments. Lighter shades suit backgrounds and logo fields; mid-tones work for subtle hover states and section contrast.
- **Petrol:** Technical counterweight to coral. Use for secondary actions, analytical context, diagrams, and structural detail.
- **Butter:** Soft warning. Communicates caution without urgency or alarm.
- **Plum:** Quiet information. Use for labels, metadata, section eyebrows, and non-primary informational states.
- **Seafoam:** Constructive success. Use for confirmations, progress indicators, and positive validation.
- **Rose:** Restrained errors. Use for destructive actions, blocking states, and critical validation.

## Usage rules

- Use `warmWhite` and `graphite` as the stable contrast pair. Do not substitute other colors for this role.
- Use `coral` sparingly — one or two accents per view. Overuse erodes its value as the signature color.
- Use `sand` for all visible structure: borders, separators, grid gaps, and subdued panels.
- Use `bg-muted` for quiet section bands and `bg-elevated` for cards that should sit visibly above them.
- Reserve `bg-raised` and full inversion for rare focus surfaces. The raised light surface is a warm off-white rather than pure white.
- Use semantic utilities (`bg-default`, `text-body`, `border-default`) in components, not raw shade classes. Raw shades require a paired `dark:` variant; semantic utilities handle both modes automatically.
- Use lighter shades (50–200) for backgrounds and tinted areas, mid shades (400–600) for text and icons on light surfaces, dark shades (700–950) for text and fills on dark surfaces.
- Do not fill large surfaces with saturated accent colors. Reserve mid-to-dark accent shades for interactive elements, not layout backgrounds.
- Dark mode uses lighter semantic shades (e.g. `coral-600` → `coral-400`) to maintain appropriate contrast on dark surfaces.
